# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary audience is **recruiters and hiring managers** evaluating Michael Real as a candidate. Secondary audience is **fellow developers and potential collaborators**. Both arrive with a specific job: assess competence, character, and fit — through projects, writing, and the overall impression the site leaves.

Context: they're scanning quickly, often in a stack of other portfolios. The site must communicate quality immediately, then reward deeper exploration.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that showcases Michael Real's work, skills, and thinking. It exists to:

- Make a strong first impression that leads to conversations (interviews, collaborations)
- Demonstrate technical competence through the projects themselves
- Communicate personality and approachability — not just a resume, but a sense of the person behind the work

Success means visitors leave with a clear understanding of what Michael builds, how he thinks, and a reason to reach out.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, approachable, human.** Three words: _grounded, competent, genuine._

The site should feel like a conversation with someone who knows their craft — not a pitch, not a flex, not a corporate brand page. Confidence comes through the work itself, not through loud design.

Emotionally: visitors should feel welcomed, not impressed upon. The tone is personal but professional — like a well-written cover letter, not a marketing landing page.

## Anti-references

- **No flashy / trendy portfolio templates** — over-animated, gradient-heavy, scroll-jacking, "look what I can do" showmanship
- **No hacker / terminal aesthetic** — green-on-black, monospace everywhere, matrix vibes, "elite" posture
- **No corporate stiffness** — rigid, impersonal, buzzword-heavy, feels like an enterprise SaaS page
- **No side-stripe borders** — colored left/right borders as card accents (the "AI slop" tell)
- **No gradient text** — background-clip gradient headlines
- **No numbered section markers** (01 / 02 / 03) as default scaffolding
- **No tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section**

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** Let the projects, the code, and the craft speak. Claims are backed by evidence on the same page.
2. **Restraint conveys taste.** Less is more. One well-chosen detail beats five competing elements. White space is confidence.
3. **Human first.** The site feels like a person, not a brand. Copy is conversational, layout is inviting, nothing feels automated or templated.
4. **Depth rewards attention.** Quick scan gives a strong impression; deeper exploration reveals more — layered information, not all-at-once.
5. **Competence is quiet.** No "rockstar" language, no overstatement. The work is good; let visitors decide.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target: **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**
- All text meets contrast requirements (≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 large text)
- Reduced motion: all animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`
- Semantic HTML, proper heading hierarchy, focus-visible indicators
- Color is never the sole differentiator for meaning
- Interactive elements meet minimum target size (24px) for touch
